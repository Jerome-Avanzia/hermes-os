# Identity Review Procedure

Standard procedure for reviewing a company's brand identity documents against
their stated acceptance criteria.

## Scope

This SOP applies when a workspace contains a set of brand identity documents
that have been produced through a structured identity-building process and
are now ready for review and approval.

The reviewer reads documents from the workspace context. Acceptance criteria
are stated within or alongside each document. This procedure does not define
what good looks like for any specific company — it defines how to evaluate
documents against criteria that already exist in the workspace.

---

## Inputs

- All identity documents referenced in the workspace `organization` block.
- Acceptance criteria, either stated within the documents or in sprint or
  project records available in the workspace.

## Output

A structured identity review report with the following sections:

1. **Review Summary** — overall status, count of Pass / Needs Revision /
   Question ratings, and one paragraph of strategic commentary.
2. **Document Reviews** — one section per document, structured by criterion.
3. **Cross-Document Consistency** — issues that only appear when documents
   are read as a system, not in isolation.
4. **Founder Questions** — a consolidated list of items that require founder
   input before the identity can be considered approved.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Load Documents

Read all identity documents from the workspace. Establish the document set:

- Foundation documents: purpose, vision, mission, values, positioning.
- Market documents: target customers, services.
- Voice document: tone of voice.
- Visual document: visual identity direction.
- Web documents: site map, homepage copy.

If a document is missing from the workspace, note it as a gap before
proceeding.

---

### Step 2 — Review Foundation Documents

For each foundation document, evaluate against these dimensions:

**Purpose**
- Does it answer: why does this company exist?
- Is it specific to this company, or could it apply to any company in the category?
- Is it free of overlap with the mission statement?

**Vision**
- Does it answer: what will this company become?
- Is it aspirational but grounded — not so abstract it gives no direction?
- Is it free of overlap with the mission statement?

**Mission**
- Does it answer: what does this company do every day?
- Does it describe action, not aspiration?
- Is the distinction between mission and vision visible to a reader who sees
  both?

**Values**
- Does each value have a name, a definition, and a behavioral statement?
- Are the values specific enough to reject a decision, or are they decorative?
- Could any value apply equally to every company in the world? If so, it is
  not a value — it is a platitude.

**Positioning**
- Does it clearly name who the company serves?
- Does it state what makes the company different from available alternatives?
- Does it make explicit what the company is not?
- Is the "for / unlike / because" logic traceable, even if not stated in that
  exact structure?

---

### Step 3 — Review Market Documents

**Target Customers**
- Does each ICP have: who they are, why the company chooses to serve them, and
  why adjacent segments are deliberately excluded?
- Is each ICP specific enough to reject a prospect?
- Do the ICPs follow logically from the positioning and business model, or do
  they read as demographic templates?

**Services**
- Does each service description lead with what the client walks away with,
  not what the company does?
- Does each service reinforce the company's positioning?
- Is there a consistent logic connecting the services to each other?

---

### Step 4 — Review Voice Document

**Tone of Voice**
- Are the voice attributes defined in terms of behavior, not adjectives only?
- Does the document include do/do-not pairs that demonstrate the voice in
  practice?
- Are sample rewrites present and do they clearly demonstrate the difference?
- Is the guidance specific enough that someone who has never written for this
  company could apply it correctly on the first attempt?

---

### Step 5 — Review Visual Identity Document

**Visual Identity Direction**
- Is every color, typeface, and logo direction justified against a named brand
  attribute? If not, the choice is arbitrary.
- Is the color palette restricted enough to be useful?
- Is the typography system complete — heading, body, mono — with rationale for
  each choice?
- Is the logo brief specific enough to brief a contractor who has never met
  the founder?
- Is the mood description concrete? Generic descriptors ("modern," "clean,"
  "professional") with no further specificity are insufficient.
- Are the "does not look like" exclusions present and specific?

---

### Step 6 — Review Web Documents

**Site Map**
- Does every page have a stated purpose, primary CTA, and content description?
- Is the navigation hierarchy logical given the company's ICPs and conversion
  goals?
- Could a developer or designer build from this without a briefing call?

**Homepage Copy**
- Is every section developed to production-ready copy — no placeholders, no
  outline-level bullets where prose is needed?
- Does the copy demonstrate the tone of voice established in the voice document?
  Spot-check at minimum three sections.
- Does the hero immediately communicate what the company is and who it serves?
- Is the final CTA specific about what happens next?

---

### Step 7 — Cross-Document Consistency Check

Read the document set as a system. Flag:

- **Terminology drift** — the same concept named differently across documents
  (e.g. "clients" in one, "customers" in another, "organizations" in a third).
- **Positioning gaps** — a differentiator claimed in the positioning document
  that is not visible anywhere in the services, tone of voice, or homepage copy.
- **Tone inconsistency** — sections of the homepage or service descriptions
  that contradict the voice document.
- **ICP misalignment** — a service or homepage section that implicitly targets
  a customer segment excluded by the ICP document.

---

### Step 8 — Compile and Deliver

Produce the review report following the output structure in the Inputs/Output
section above. Deliver it in a format the founder can read, annotate, and
respond to directly.

Do not summarise what the documents say. The reviewer has read them. Begin
each document section with the rating summary, then the supporting detail.

---

## Rating Definitions

**Pass** — The criterion is fully met. The document text provides clear
evidence. No changes required.

**Needs Revision** — The criterion is partially met or contains a correctable
defect. State exactly what is missing or wrong, and what a corrected version
would include.

**Question** — The criterion cannot be evaluated without founder input. State
specifically what is unclear and what needs to be decided before the review
can be completed.

---

## Completion Criteria

The review is complete when:

- Every document has been evaluated against every applicable criterion.
- Every Needs Revision item has a specific, actionable recommendation.
- Every Question item is consolidated in the Founder Questions section.
- The Review Summary reflects the full state of the identity as a system.
